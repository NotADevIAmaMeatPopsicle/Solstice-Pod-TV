Solstice Pod TV

Giving a retired Mersive Solstice Pod Gen3 (SP8096) a second life as a 4K Android 15 TV box.

APQ8096 · Snapdragon 820 Android 15 · LineageOS 22.2 Linux 4.4.302-podmin+ HDMI-CEC + 4K@60 Hz

Surplus Mersive Solstice Pod Gen3 (SP8096) units are compact matte-black plastic presentation boxes stuck on Android 7.1.1 and Linux 3.18. Inside sits a Snapdragon 820, 4 GB RAM, 16 GB eMMC, dual HDMI outputs (native 4K@60 Hz + 1080p60), Gigabit Ethernet with PoE+, two USB 3.0 ports, QCA6174A Wi-Fi/Bluetooth, a 3.5 mm jack, and an HDMI-in bridge.

Self-booting Android 15 (LineageOS 22.2) with Projectivy Launcher and msm_watchdog_v2 is running on physical hardware with 4K@60 Hz HDMI out (auto-reconnecting on hot-plug or TV input switch), Venus 4K hardware video decode, 5 GHz Wi-Fi (ath10k_pci), Bluetooth A2DP, Gigabit Ethernet, 3.5 mm stereo audio, native HDMI-CEC TV remote control, and Flirc IR remote verified on a living room TV.

Inside the box

The first surprise when you start working on a Mersive Solstice Pod Gen3 (SP8096): there are no external reset or fastboot buttons anywhere on the case. To reach the internal VOL- (S3) button for fastboot, or the unpopulated P9 UART (RX/TX/GND) serial header, you have to open the plastic shell and lift the board (ASSY_003304_REVP2) out. Once it's open, though, it's a really clean board to work on. Jump down to the teardown & flashing walkthrough for the exact screws and button steps.

Non-brick rule: Never fastboot flash boot an untested kernel, and never put an untested insmod in on boot. Always RAM-boot via fastboot boot first, test new .ko modules live from /data/local/tmp/, and only automate verified modules under on property:sys.boot_completed=1.

The bring-up story

Getting a 2016-era APQ8096 appliance from stock Android 7.1.1 (Linux 3.18) to Android 15 (LineageOS 22.2) on Linux 4.4.302 came down to five real-world fights:

1Staying under the 32 MB (0x2000000) bootloader wall

The APQ8096 Little Kernel (aboot) silently refuses to boot if the uncompressed kernel + DTB crosses 0x2000000. Starting from Qualcomm's minimal msm_defconfig (dropping LG phone drivers, UFS, battery/charger, NFC, touchscreens, and cnss) kept us safely under the ceiling, enforced by check-image-layout.py.

2Pairing the right LineageOS 22.2 donors

Official LineageOS 22.2 for the LG G5 (h850) provided the MSM8996 Android 15 system, Adreno 530 GPU, and Venus 4K decoder base (with phone-only HALs stripped), while official LineageOS 22.2 for the Xiaomi Mi 5 (gemini) supplied native QTI Wi-Fi and Bluetooth HALs for the Pod's QCA6174A / Rome radio.

3Finding TLMM GPIO 127 for native 4K HDMI 1

HDMI 2 (ADV7533) came up at 1080p60 while HDMI 1 (9a0000.qcom,hdmi_tx) looked dead. Diffing the stock DTB turned up TLMM GPIO 127. Driving it HIGH on boot and enabling Display 2 after boot locked straight onto 4K@60 Hz (594 MHz pixel clock) on a living room TV.

4Ditching qcacld-3.0 and catching a 1-line crash in ath10k

Stock qcacld-3.0 kept SSR-crashing on Linux 4.4.302. Switching to upstream ath10k_pci enumerated the card cleanly, then immediately panicked at 0x0000001c in ath10k_ce_send_nolock. Live dmesg -w pointed straight to ce.c:491: a CAF WCN3990 backport dereferenced vaddr_rri_on_ddr[ce_id] without checking if (ar->rri_on_ddr) (NULL[7] = 0x1c). Patching that line (0017), closing an MSI unmask race + disabling pci_ps/ASPM (0018), loading WLAN.RM.4.4.1 (firmware-5.bin), and adding an 8-line wlan.ko shim for /sys/module/wlan/parameters/fwpath brought 5 GHz Wi-Fi up at 866 Mbps Rx, 0% loss alongside Bluetooth A2DP.

5Couch control with native HDMI-CEC (and Flirc USB)

We started out with a Flirc USB Gen2 and an old Rosewill Media Center IR remote on the rear USB 3.0 port. Later we spotted a single #define BLOCK_CEC line LG had left in mdss_hdmi_tx.c. Removing that line (0021) brought Qualcomm's native fb1/cec controller online on HDMI 1, and our lightweight pod-cec-daemon (running on logical address 8 so it never collides with consoles on LA 4 or steals TV inputs) turns a regular TV remote into native Android navigation over /dev/uinput.

Teardown & flashing walkthrough

Once you have built the three images (pod44-los22-boot-signed.img, pod44-los22-recovery-signed.img, pod-los22-system.simg) from docs/BUILD.md and have Android Platform Tools (adb and fastboot, plus the Google Android Bootloader Interface USB driver on Windows), getting a stock Mersive Solstice Pod Gen3 (SP8096) onto Android 15 takes about 5–10 minutes.

1Take off the bottom cover (4 screws)

Unplug power and any cables, flip the Pod over, and peel back the 4 rubber feet in the corners. Take out the 4 screws underneath and lift the bottom cover off.

2Pull the board out and set the aluminum block aside (6 screws)

Once the bottom cover is off, you'll see 6 screws holding the board in place (4 in the corners and 2 in the middle, marked above). Take those out and the board is free to lift right out. That aluminum heatsink block isn't bolted to the board. It's only held on with a light layer of thermal paste, and mine fell right off while I was pulling the board out. Just set the aluminum block aside so you can see and reach the buttons on the top of the board.

3Hold VOL- (S3) while powering on to enter Fastboot

Plug a USB-C data cable into the Pod's front USB-C port and connect it to your PC (the rear USB 3.0 ports won't work for fastboot or ADB). Find the VOL- (S3) button on the top side of the board, just to the right of the USB-C port (between VOL+ and PWR).

Press and hold VOL- (S3), plug in 12 V power (or PoE+), keep holding it for about 3–5 seconds, and then let go. Check that your PC sees the Pod:

fastboot devices

You should see a device ID followed by fastboot. If nothing shows up on Windows, check Device Manager for an unknown Android device and assign it the Google Android Bootloader Interface driver.

4Back up stock partitions and flash Android 15

If you haven't backed up this Pod yet, save copies of boot, recovery, persist, modem, and bluetooth first. Launch the cross-platform GUI flasher on Windows, macOS, or Linux (live Pod status, clean install, keep-data upgrade, progress bar, and SHA-256 backup/restore):

python3 tools/pod-flasher/pod_flasher.py

Or run the Windows PowerShell CLI installer directly:

powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\installer\flash-new-pod.ps1 `
  -ImagesDir .\out\images `
  -PlatformToolsDir .\platform-tools `
  -ApksDir .\out\tv-apks

The script flashes boot, recovery, and system, formats userdata and cache as ext4, reboots into Android 15, applies the TV defaults (hides the battery icon, locks landscape, disables leftover phone apps), installs any APKs in ApksDir, and runs a pass/fail verification check before marking the unit done in flash-state.json. It is resumable (if interrupted after flashing, re-running it picks up over ADB), supports -Batch for flashing multiple units back-to-back, and never touches persist, modem, or bt_firmware, so each Pod keeps its factory MAC addresses and calibration. Read docs/SAFETY.md before flashing.

5Put it back together and hook it up to your TV

Unplug power and USB-C, set the aluminum heatsink block back onto the board (making sure the thermal paste lines up cleanly), and put the 6 board screws back in. Snap the bottom cover back on with the 4 case screws and press the 4 rubber feet back in place. Plug HDMI 1 (the rightmost rear HDMI port) into your 4K TV, plug a USB keyboard/mouse or Flirc USB receiver into one of the rear USB 3.0 ports, and power it up.
